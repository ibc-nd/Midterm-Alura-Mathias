c) Explain in 2–3 sentences what the E-value represents and why we use a threshold.
#Mathias Reponse:
The E-value is a measure of statistical significance of how close the test sequence is to the reference sequence. 
It represents the number of matches with a score at least as good as the observed match that would be expected to occur by chance (false positive). 
We use this as a threshold to filter out matches that are likely to be random rather than significant.
A lower E-value indicates a more statistically significant match.

### 5 (10 pts)
a) Describe the difference between **pairwise alignment** and **profile-based searching**. When would you use each?
#Mathias response:
Pairwise alignment means arranging two sequences and comparing them directly to identify which regions have similarities that line up. 
On the other hand profile-based searching uses a hidden markov model (HMM) profile built from multiple sequence alignments of similar sequences that are related to capture which positions are conserved. 
However, it also is able to capture the variation within a protein family, which pairwise alignment struggles with. 
Pairwise alignment is useful when comparing closely related sequences. 
Profile-based searching is useful for identifying distantly related sequences that may be missed by pairwise alignment, as it is more flexible with point mutations.

b) Why is building an HMM profile from multiple sequences more sensitive than using a single sequence as a BLAST query?
#Mathias response:
An HMM profile allows for conserved positions, variable regions, and patterns of AA substitutions to be captured across multiple related sequences. 
This can allow for recognition of more distant homologs, as it has more sequences to compare it to. 
A single-sequence BLAST query doesn’t capture the full range of variation that can be seen in a proteome, making it less sensitive for detecting variability and overall more distant evolutionary relationships.
